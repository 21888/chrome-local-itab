# Offline world clocks

In Settings → Appearance → World clocks, enter an IANA time zone (for example America/New_York or Asia/Kathmandu) and an optional short label. The suggestion list is just a starting point; any named zone recognized by your browser is supported. Select Add clock, then Save clocks (or Save settings). Remove a clock and save to remove it from the dashboard. Use Move up or Move down to change the display order. Add, remove and reorder only change the draft until an explicit save. Keyboard focus follows the moved clock; at an end of the list it switches to the available direction. A maximum of four different zones and 40 Unicode characters per label is supported.

Saved clock changes update an already-open homepage without reloading or discarding its drafts.

Clocks appear as a compact information card when at least one is saved and Show clock is enabled. They inherit the existing 12/24-hour and seconds settings. Today, Yesterday and Tomorrow compare the zone’s calendar date with your device’s calendar date; the most distant zones may show +2 or -2 days. Time-zone rules come from your browser’s Intl database. No location request, network request, stored offset, new permission or external service is involved.

World clocks are ordinary configuration: JSON export/import, Google Drive backup and optional Chrome Sync include them in their saved order. Older backups default to no world clocks; resetting settings clears them. Invalid lists in an import are rejected. Local private Tasks, Focus and Scratchpad content keeps its existing independent behavior.

An unrelated Settings auto-save does not apply your pending world-clock changes. Another page’s clock change causes a clear save conflict rather than overwriting it: keep your draft visible and open a new Settings tab to review the latest values. Unsaved clock drafts delay automatic reloads; intentional reload can discard them after confirmation. A browser departure warning is best effort. No unload-time saving is attempted.

Choose **Compare times** on the world-clock card to preview a call time. Use **Back 24 hours** / **Forward 24 hours** to keep exploring earlier or later dates without restarting comparison. Each step moves exactly 24 elapsed hours and preserves the slider setting and the originally captured seconds. The unchanged −24 to +24-hour slider adjusts this stepped base in 15-minute increments; the original captured-now reference reports total minutes from the instant you opened comparison. Across daylight saving, 24 elapsed hours may reach a different local clock hour. The preview stays fixed while the main clock, calendar and timers remain live. The local reference includes date, time and time-zone abbreviation; Same day / Previous day / Next day (or signed day counts) are relative to that reference date. Each comparison row also shows its own Gregorian date as `YYYY-MM-DD`, so year-end and date-line previews need no date conversion. Daylight-saving changes and half-/quarter-hour zones use the browser’s time-zone rules. Use the slider’s arrow keys or Home/End, and choose **Back to now** to restore live world clocks. Navigation is limited to Gregorian years 0001–9999, with conservative edge dates reserved so the entire slider window remains safe after a time-zone change. An unavailable direction stays focusable and is marked unavailable. Comparison is only on this page: no save, sync, backup, network request or added permission. Clock setting/template changes preserve the preview while the card exists; removing all clocks or reloading clears it.

You can also enter a **Device-local date and time** and choose **Preview** (or press plain Enter in the field). Editing alone does not apply it. The date is interpreted in the device IANA zone shown beside the field, with optional seconds (omitted seconds become `00`; milliseconds become zero). Invalid dates and local times skipped by daylight saving are rejected without changing the existing preview or rewriting the field. Repeated local times use the earlier occurrence, as the help explains before submission. The applied reference includes the device IANA zone and its actual numeric GMT offset, including historical offset seconds when needed. Preview resets the comparison base and both offsets to zero; subsequent slider and 24-hour steps use elapsed time from that base and keep its seconds. The reference then reports minutes from the current comparison base. The original captured-now path retains its “from start” reference and captured milliseconds.

Drafts and errors survive ticks, display-format/language/order/template changes and hide/show while the card exists. A device-zone change keeps the already-applied instant: an untouched, unfocused field may refresh to the new zone, but an edited or focused field is preserved and asks you to edit again before applying in the new zone. If Preview detects a zone change before the next refresh, it also keeps the field and requires another edit. Returning to now, removing all clocks or recreating the page clears the field and error. Copy always uses the applied visible result, never a pending draft. This does not save, schedule or invite anyone. [Local date/time entry checks and native-test limits](world-clock-time-entry-validation.md).

In comparison mode, choose **Copy comparison** to copy the local reference followed by each displayed row, in order, as `label · time · relative day · YYYY-MM-DD`. This uses exactly the displayed text, including its language, clock format and labels. A separate status confirms success or explains how to select and copy the text manually if the clipboard API is unavailable or denied. It does not create a calendar invitation: the rows retain their displayed relative days and full dates, without adding IANA zone identifiers to individual rows (the entered-time reference includes the device zone). Back to now restores relative-only live rows. Changing the preview or its display retires any pending feedback; an already-issued clipboard write cannot be cancelled.
